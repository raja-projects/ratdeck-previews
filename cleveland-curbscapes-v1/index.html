Why homeowners switch

Tired of messy mulch edges and shifting plastic borders?

Plastic and timber edging move, rot and warp. Deep edging only holds its shape for a short time. A CurbScapes curb is concrete, so it stays where we pour it.

Plastic or timber edging

  • Shifts and pulls out of place
  • Timber rots and warps
  • Mulch and stone end up on the lawn

Deep edging by hand

  • Only holds its shape for a short time
  • Has to be re-cut every season
  • Grass creeps back into the beds
CurbScapes curb

Concrete landscape curbing

  • Solid border keeps grass and weeds out of your beds
  • Keeps the mulch in the beds and off the grass
  • Won't rot or warp, with wire reinforcement inside
  • Saves time every time you cut and trim the grass
  • Rinse with a hose, reseal every 12 to 18 months

    From a recent CurbScapes job

    Two colors in every curb

    You pick a Primary Color for the surface and an Integral Curb Color for the concrete itself. You see roughly 80% primary and 20% integral, which gives the curb its natural, stone-like depth.

    Installation day

    One day, start to sealed.

    Most of this gets covered when Doug or Mike comes out to quote. Here is what happens on the day for most jobs.

    01

    Site prep

    We cut clean bed edges and clear roots, grass and anything that could crack the curb later. We walk the layout with you before a drop of concrete goes down.

    02

    Mix & extrude

    Concrete is mixed fresh on site and pushed through our curbing machine in one continuous run, following the exact curve of your beds. If anything needs to change, we talk to you first.

    03

    Finish & seal

    Your finish is textured, stamped or hand-carved, then sealed the same day. The sealer protects the color for up to two years and resists mold, mildew and UV.

    All we need from you

    A water supply and a safe place to park our trucks. That's it.

    Looking after it

    Rinse with a garden hose as needed. Have us reseal every 18 months, and skip store-bought sealers, which can damage colored concrete.
